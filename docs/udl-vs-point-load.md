# UDL vs Point Load on Shelving: What a Shelf Rating Really Means

A shelf rating such as **200kg UDL** describes one specific situation: the weight spread evenly across the whole shelf. Most real shelves are not loaded that way, which is why a shelf can bow under far less than its rated figure.

## UDL in one sentence

**UDL (uniformly distributed load)** is weight spread evenly across the full length and depth of a shelf. A 200kg UDL shelf is designed for 200kg laid edge to edge, not 200kg resting on one spot.

## Point load in one sentence

A **point load** (or concentrated load) is weight sitting on a small area, such as an engine block, a stack of tiles or a single heavy toolbox in the middle of the span.

## Why the difference matters

| Same 200kg on a 2m shelf | What the steel experiences |
|---|---|
| Spread across the shelf (UDL) | Bending is shared along the full span |
| Sitting in the middle (point load) | Bending concentrates at mid-span, the weakest point |

There is no single conversion from UDL to point load that applies to every shelf; it depends on the shelf design, span and supports, and only the manufacturer can give a true point-load figure. Without one, the [Shelving Load Calculator](https://rackingoutlet.github.io/shelving-load-calculator/) uses **half the UDL rating** for concentrated loads as a cautious working assumption.

## Practical ways to turn a point load into a UDL

1. **Spread it out.** Put the heavy item on a sheet of plywood or MDF so the weight reaches the shelf across a wider area.
2. **Move it towards the uprights.** The ends of a shelf, near the supports, bend far less than the middle.
3. **Use the bottom shelf.** Heavy concentrated items are safest at floor level.
